X16 / OpenSRS — original procedural pistol sound set

STATUS
16 individual sound assets: all 9 required sound types (11 WAVs because the
shot has 3 variations), plus all 5 optional effects.
Technical checks pass. Subjective listening is PENDING: the authoring session
cannot receive audio input. No claim is made that these were heard or that
listening established realism. Use preview/X16_Audition_Reel.wav to assess
whether the gunshots and slide mechanics sound convincingly natural.

DELIVERY
48,000 Hz, mono, signed 24-bit PCM WAV. Each individual asset is trimmed to
its active signal and peak-normalized to approximately -1 dBTP using 4x
oversampling, then independently checked at 8x. Sample peaks can be lower
than -1 dBFS because reconstructed peaks between samples can be higher.
No hard clipping or brick-wall limiter is used. A few samples of attack
ramping and short tail fades prevent edit clicks; these are not leading
silence. TPDF dither is applied only at 24-bit quantization scale.
All audio is newly synthesized from equations and seeded random numbers.
There are no recordings, borrowed samples, library effects, sampled impulse
responses, voices, or audio-generation-model outputs anywhere in the set.
The Python libraries perform signal processing; they provide no audio content.

HOW EACH SOUND WAS MADE
X16_Gunshot_01.wav
X16_Gunshot_02.wav
X16_Gunshot_03.wav
  Three independent seeded renders of the same close-shot design. An analytic
  bipolar pressure pulse creates the leading crack. Brief high-frequency
  noise adds the shock texture; separate rapidly decaying midrange gas and
  low-mid pressure bands add weight. Very quiet short mechanical contacts
  suggest the action cycle. Randomized, filtered early reflections and a
  low diffuse tail provide restrained outdoor space. Timing and pressure
  decay vary slightly between shots; these are not pitch-shifted duplicates.
  No sustained sub-bass oscillator, dramatic echo, or musical resonator.
  This is an approximate sound-design model, not a measured recording of a
  particular firearm or a calibrated physical acoustic simulation.

X16_DryFire.wav
  A tight striker/sear contact, a quieter secondary contact, and heavily
  damped polymer/steel modes. The short decay avoids a toy-like ringing ping.

X16_MagazineRelease.wav
  A small button contact, latch snap, then faint irregular polymer friction
  as the magazine begins to move. The floor impact is a separate asset.

X16_MagazineInsert.wav
  A light guiding contact and brief friction lead into a heavier seated
  polymer thud, a sharper catch engagement, and a small settling contact.

X16_SlidePullBack.wav
  An initial engagement click, irregular steel-rail friction, a very quiet
  inharmonic spring/rail response, and a heavier rear stop with tiny settling
  contacts. The strongest stop occurs about 131 ms after the start.

X16_SlideForward.wav
  A brief release and faster rail movement terminate in a layered steel
  closure with closely spaced contacts. The main closure is about 34 ms
  after the start. Short, damped modes avoid a sustained spring boing.

X16_Impact_Concrete.wav
  A dry, bright fractured transient, short broad stone-body resonance, and
  tiny irregular grit contacts. No stylized ricochet whistle.

X16_Impact_Body.wav
  Strongly damped low-mid noise for soft mass, a brief fabric snap, and a
  quiet secondary contact. No vocal material or exaggerated wet splatter.

X16_HitMarker_Tick.wav
  A very short bright noise transient with faint, quickly damped inharmonic
  partials. Intended as restrained non-spatial player feedback, not a chime.

OPTIONAL
X16_GunshotDistant.wav
  Derived only from a freshly rendered original shot. Reduced bandwidth
  removes the close crack; a few softened delayed copies broaden the tail.

X16_NearMissWhiz.wav
  A brief noise pass with a moving broad spectral emphasis and a tiny
  pressure transient. No sinusoidal or cartoon-style whistle.

X16_DrawRustle.wav
  Irregular filtered-noise grains model fabric/polymer friction, supported
  by two light, heavily damped contacts.

X16_MagazineFloor.wav
  A weighted polymer/metal impact, irregular diminishing bounces, and a
  short friction scrape. Intended for a hard floor.

X16_CasingTinkle.wav
  Brief inharmonic brass-like modes, a small broadband contact, and several
  diminishing bounces. It is intentionally the most resonant small effect.

PREVIEW AND MIXING
preview/X16_Audition_Reel.wav contains every asset in the order above, with
pauses. preview/CUE_SHEET.json supplies timestamps and applied gains.
The reel is deliberately quieter than the normalized individual files.
Peak normalization does NOT imply that all game sounds should play at the
same volume. Start with gunshots as the loud reference; keep dry fire,
magazine movements, and slides lower, with casing and clothing much lower.
The preview gains are audition starting points, not a finished game mix.
Randomize between the 3 close shots rather than applying large pitch shifts.
Use the distant version as a distance-dependent replacement or crossfade;
avoid stacking it at full level with a nearby shot. The environmental tail
is intentionally modest so the game can add location-specific acoustics.
Spatialize the weapon and impact sounds in the game. Keep the hit marker
centered and quiet. Do not play the optional floor/casing effects inside the
reload sounds as well; they are provided separately for animation events.
Leave additional mixer headroom when several assets play simultaneously.

REPRODUCIBILITY AND QA
source/synthesize.py contains the complete synthesis and fixed random seeds.
source/requirements.txt lists the actual generation-library versions.
source/validate.py repeats file, trimming, finite-sample, DC, clipping,
8x true-peak, and shot-variation checks. VALIDATION.json records the results
and explicitly distinguishes those checks from listening.
SHA256SUMS.txt identifies the delivered files.
The existing model assets and their source file were not modified.
